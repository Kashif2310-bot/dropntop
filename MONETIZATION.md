# drop'n'top — monetization design

Core philosophy (Kashif's framing): never charge before someone feels the product is genuinely useful. The paywall should appear at the moment of felt relief or habit, priced so low that paying feels like an obvious trade against the value already delivered — not a toll on entry.

## Two different buyers, two different triggers

**Individual users (exam-form bundle, general drop/retrieve)**
- Free tier must be generous enough to form a habit: unlimited drops, reasonable file size (e.g. 200MB/file), 24h expiry, up to 5 retrievals per drop. No signup required for a single drop.
- Never show a price on first use. The first drop and first retrieve must be completely frictionless — that first "wow, it just worked, full quality, no WhatsApp" moment is the entire acquisition engine.
- Track quiet engagement signals per device/account: drops in last 30 days, successful retrievals, exam-form compressions used.
- Fire the upgrade prompt at one of two precise moments, never randomly:
  1. **Right after a successful retrieve** (the user is relieved/happy, not frustrated) on someone's 4th or 5th use: "You've used drop'n'top 5 times this month. Go Pro for ₹49/month — that's less than one printout run — and never think about limits again."
  2. **At the exact point of friction**: file too big, drop expired before the recipient grabbed it, or accessor limit hit. Frame the upgrade as preventing the *next* frustration, not fixing this one retroactively (never hold a file hostage — that breaks trust instantly and reads as a scam pattern).
- Anchor every price against something small and concrete the user already spends money on: one printout, one data recharge, one auto-rickshaw ride — never against competitor pricing, which means nothing to a first-time Indian consumer.
- Exam season: a one-time ₹19-29 "exam form pack" (unlimited compress+drop for 30 days) sold as an impulse buy during CUET/SSC/board season — cheaper than a subscription for someone who only needs this once or twice a year, but still real revenue at scale given seasonal volume.

**Shop/PG/institution accounts (print shop, PG/hostel, CSC vertical)**
- The end customer (student dropping a file, tenant submitting ID) never pays. The shop or PG owner pays, because they're the one who benefits from a clean dashboard and no personal WhatsApp exposure.
- Owner gets a free code/QR poster from day one — no card required to try it. Paywall triggers only once they've had enough real volume to feel the pain of the free tier's cap (e.g., more than 15 drops/month, or wanting drop history beyond 7 days).
- Price monthly, small, and framed against what they're already losing: "₹149/month — less than the time you spend each week hunting for someone's file in WhatsApp."

## Guardrails
- Never lock a user out of a file they already have a valid code for — expiry and limits are set by the person who dropped it, not used as a lever to force payment on the retriever.
- Every upgrade prompt must be dismissible in one tap and never repeat more than once per session.
- No dark patterns (fake urgency, hidden auto-renewal surprises) — trust is the entire product; violating it once undermines both verticals that depend on trust (PG document exchange, exam form submission).
